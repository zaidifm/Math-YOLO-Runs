# Jacobian Blackboard Checkpoints 118–120

## 118 — `SL_2/T` itself has the wrong canonical class for a plane Keller normalization

For
\[
R=k[W,b,c]/(cW-b(b-1)),
\]
`R` is a smooth affine hypersurface, so `K_R=0`. Localizing at `W` is a UFD; the two primes over `W=0` are `D_0=(W,b)` and `D_1=(W,b-1)` with principal relation `D_0+D_1=div(W)`. Nagata localization gives `Cl(R) ~= Z`, generated by the missing color `D_0`. The hidden big cell contains `D_1` and omits `D_0`.

If a finite map `R -> A^2` were nontrivial and étale on that big cell, its ramification divisor would be a positive multiple of `D_0`. Riemann–Hurwitz would force `K_R=R_pi`, contradicting `K_R=0` and `[D_0]!=0`. Thus the two-dimensional coadjoint orbit cannot itself be the finite normalization of a plane Keller counterexample. The extra dimension is what supplies the missing canonical/ramification class.

## 119 — the primitive degree-three plane architecture is the `S_3` reflection quotient

For the depressed cubic
\[
f(T)=T^3-3aT-b,
\]
the chosen-root normalization is `A^2_{z,a}` with `b=z^3-3az`; its Jacobian is `-3(z^2-a)`. Its simple-root open is therefore `A^2 \ {a=z^2} ~= A^1 x G_m`, not `A^2`.

The Galois closure is the Weyl/reflection representation of `S_3` on `A^2_{s,t}` with basic invariants
\[
a=st,\qquad b=s^3+t^3.
\]
The target is `A^2/S_3 ~= A^2`; quotient by one reflection subgroup gives the intermediate cubic normalization `A^2`. The discriminant double cover is
\[
r^2=b^2-4a^3,
\]
which becomes the `A_2` singularity `UV=a^3`; its canonical `mu_3` quasi-étale cover is `U=s^3,V=t^3,a=st`.

Thus degree-three monodromy is not mysterious: the reflection model is explicit. What fails is boundary geometry. Its ramification curve is principal, so deleting it introduces a nonconstant unit. An actual JC2 counterexample would have to **deprincipalize/split** that ramification boundary while preserving finiteness and the affine-plane open.

## 120 — the Fable cubic is a second-jet strict transform of that reflection cover

Start with
\[
g(z;P,Q)=z^3-3Pz+Q.
\]
Around the simple branch point `(P,Q,z)=(1,2,1)`, introduce
\[
z=1+3Wa,
\]
\[
P=1+3UW,
\]
\[
Q=2+9UW+54SW^2.
\]
Exact substitution gives
\[
\boxed{g=27W^2(Wa^3+a^2-Ua+2S).}
\]
The derivative has only one forced factor:
\[
\boxed{g_z=9W(3Wa^2+2a-U).}
\]
And the cubic discriminant transforms as
\[
\boxed{4P^3-Q^2=27W^2\Delta,}
\]
where `Delta` is the Fable nonproperness discriminant.

At `W=0`, the depressed cubic is `(z-1)^2(z+2)`. Hence the extra dimension is a jet/blow-up parameter at a simple `2+1` branch configuration: root displacement occurs at order `W`, base motion is tangent to the discriminant at order `W`, true transverse motion appears at order `W^2`, and dividing out those forced factors converts ordinary ramification into the nonproper boundary behavior of the three-dimensional Keller map.
